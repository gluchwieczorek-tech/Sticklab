Learn

What parents actually need to know — measuring, suspenders, the zipper and knee overlap.

1 · Measuring guide + coverage check

Measure the waist where the pant actually sits — just above the hip bone, snug but not tight. Height without skates. And mind the trap: pant size ≠ jeans size (Bauer e.g. tells adults to subtract ~3 in from their normal trouser measure) — measure the child, not the clothing label.

With the pants on (ideally with shin guards): 1) Tailbone — padding covers the whole tailbone, also in a skating crouch. 2) Hips — hard caps right over the hip bone. 3) Kidneys — kidney pads sit over the kidneys and meet the shoulder pad's back plate. 4) Knee — pant hem 2–5 cm above the kneecap, outside the shin guard top.

2 · Suspenders vs belt for slim kids

On narrow hips a belted pant slides down mid-session: the caps rotate and the tailbone/kidneys sit exposed — exactly when it matters. Hockey suspenders keep the protection positioned regardless of waist size; most junior pants have suspender buttons.

Rule of thumb: slim build, or waist at the low end of the size → suspenders. Tighten the belt as well, not instead.

3 · The +1″ length-extension zipper

Many models hide a zipper (often under the kidney belt) that releases +1 inch ≈ 2.5 cm of length — free growth room and fine-tuning of knee overlap. Confirmed on: CCM Jetspeed / Jetspeed + / Jetspeed Pro (JR) and Warrior Alpha LX 20 (JR). Bauer's current Performance/Elite/Pro pants advertise no extension. See the badge on every catalogue card.

Buying tip: if the child is between sizes in height — pick the one that locks the waist and take the length from the zipper.

4 · The gap to the shin guard

Standing, the pant hem should end 2–5 cm above the kneecap and sit outside the shin guard top. In a hockey stance the thigh must never go bare. Too long: the pant pushes the shin guard down and catches at the knee; too short: bare thigh and a knee gap — the bruise zone for shots and sticks. Always test in a skating crouch, shin guards on.

5 · Girdle vs classic pant

A girdle is compression-fit protection plus a separate shell. In junior sizes there's e.g. Bauer ACP (Pro/Elite) and Warrior's Alpha girdle. It hugs and slides less, but it's two pieces to buy and a tighter fit. The classic pant is the standard for kids in Norway; the girdle is the niche pick for players who hate a “floating” pant.

6 · Rules in Norway + care

NIHF follows the IIHF rules: pants are standard protective equipment, and protection must (with few exceptions like gloves and helmet) be worn under the uniform (rule 210). There are no pant-specific size or certification requirements. The kids-hockey minimum list explicitly names full-cage helmet, shin guards, neck guard, skates and stick (match regulations).

Care: air-dry after every session with zippers/velcro open. Hand wash or gentle 30°C without spin when needed. Never tumble-dry — the foams deform.
