3 · The rules in Norway (NIHF)

NIHF: every player up to and including U18 must wear full facial protection (cage or full visor). In child hockey (U13 and younger), helmet with cage, neck guard and shin guards are mandatory equipment. The helmet must meet international standards and be worn with the chin strap fastened. All players must wear a neck guard; U20 must additionally wear a mouthguard.

Sources:NIHF Spilleregler (hockey.no, ver. 30.07.2025)NIHF Kampreglement (rev. 24.03.2025)NIHF Dommerhefte Nivå 1 (hockey.no)

1 · How to measure the head

schematic sketch
  1. Use a soft measuring tape (or string + ruler).
  2. Place it about one finger width / 2 cm above the eyebrows, over the ears, taking the longest path around the back of the head.
  3. Read in whole/half cm. Measure twice - kids’ heads and tapes both move.
  4. Compare with the model’s chart. If the measurement fits two sizes: pick the smaller.

2 · Certifications explained

CE
Europa / Norge (EØS)
No printed expiry

The legal requirement in Norway. Helmets sold in the EEA must be CE-marked to the European ice-hockey protection standard (EN ISO 10256). Organized hockey requires a CE-marked helmet. Manufacture date is marked inside - CE has no printed expiry, but the foam still ages.

HECC
USA
Has an expiry date

US certification (required by USA Hockey). The HECC sticker carries a PRINTED EXPIRY DATE: 6.5 years after manufacture. Expired, illegible or removed label = no longer certified. That date is the best rule of thumb for foam lifespan - in Norway too.

CSA
Canada
No printed expiry

Canadian standard (CSA Z262.1). The label shows year of manufacture but no expiry. Canadian guidance is still to replace after roughly 6-7 years.

4 · When must the helmet be replaced?

  • Immediately after a concussion or one hard hit - the foam is single-use for big impacts.
  • After ~6-7 years regardless: HECC sets expiry at 6.5 years after manufacture, and foam ages just as fast in Norway. The manufacture date is marked in/on the helmet.
  • With shell cracks, loose screws/rivets, compressed foam or a dull, scratched-up surface.
  • When the head has outgrown the range - a too-small helmet is also the wrong helmet.

5 · The fit test (in store or at home)

  1. Front rim one finger width above the eyebrows - not up on the forehead, not down in the eyes.
  2. Open the adjustment fully, put the helmet on, tighten until snug - without pressure points.
  3. Shake the head: the helmet must not shift or lift.
  4. Chin strap snug but comfortable; the cage’s chin cup should rest on the chin without pressing.
  5. Ears centred in the ear holes. All good: buy. Anything off: other size or model.

6 · Used helmet = no

  • Foam degrades invisibly - a helmet can look fine and be finished.
  • You do not know its impact history. One hard hit is enough.
  • The certification may have expired (check the HECC date) or the labels removed - then the helmet is formally not approved.
  • This is the one piece of gear where "hand-me-down" is bad advice. Buy new - the budget models in the catalogue are approved protection.

7 · Independent testing (Virginia Tech)

CE and HECC are pass/fail minimums for a helmet to be sold. The Virginia Tech Helmet Lab goes further: independent, free of manufacturer funding, ranking helmets 0-5 stars by measured ability to reduce concussion risk.

  • In December 2025 the lab released its first dedicated youth hockey helmet ratings: 33 models, tested with the cage mounted and with a youth-sized dummy and youth impact energies.
  • 10 helmets earned 5 stars and 6 earned 4. The lab recommends choosing a 4- or 5-star helmet.
  • Certified is not the same as best: two CE/HECC-approved helmets can protect differently, and an approved helmet can still score low at VT.
  • Look up your model (exact name) in the lists below - the youth and senior lists are separate and their scores are not comparable across lists. Per-model stars are not in our catalogue yet.

Adjustment systems - in short

Every current helmet in the catalogue adjusts tool-free: CCM Tacks uses clips/flaps in the shell, Bauer RE-AKT uses central adjustment (plus FreeForm length/width on some models), and the Warrior CF 100 has Snap Flaps plus inserts you heat-mould in a home oven for a near-custom fit. Older models (e.g. Bauer 5100) may need a screwdriver. Kids’ combos (Tacks 70 YTH/JR) also have a height-adjustable cage.
